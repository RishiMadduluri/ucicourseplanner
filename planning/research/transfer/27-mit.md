# Massachusetts Institute of Technology — transfer research

Retrieved 2026-08-31.
Sources: https://mitadmissions.org/apply/transfer/ , /before-applying/ , /deadlines/

## Eligibility
- The transfer application is for students who finished high school and **will have completed at
  least one year of college before entering MIT**.
- **Minimum two terms** of full-time study with high academic standing at an accredited college,
  university, technical institute, or community college. In your first year of college, the earliest
  you can apply is during your **second term**.
- **Maximum five terms** — students who will have completed more than two and a half years of
  college may be ineligible because of MIT's residency requirements.
- **One application per entry year.** Applying for spring 2027 bars a fall 2027 application.
- MIT does not award second bachelor's degrees.

## *** Recommended coursework — MIT's own list ***
> "To prepare for MIT's academic program, we recommend that transfer students complete the
>  following college courses before entering MIT:"
> - **Single variable calculus**
> - **Multivariable calculus**
> - **Calculus-based physics**
> - **Introductory biology**
> - **Introductory chemistry**
> "If you received college credit through advanced exams (AP, IB, etc.), then you should enroll at
>  the appropriate level for each of the disciplines mentioned above."
This mirrors MIT's General Institute Requirements science core: two semesters of college calculus,
two of calculus-based physics, one of chemistry, one of biology.

## GPA
> "There is no minimum required GPA, but competitive applicants generally have a GPA of **3.5 or
>  above, and mostly A's in mathematics and science courses**."

## Credit transfer
- Admitted transfers receive credit for subjects **substantively equivalent** to MIT subjects.
- **"Transfer students typically lose at least one semester of coursework, and most students enter
  MIT as sophomores — regardless of the amount of coursework they completed."**
- Credit is assessed only after admission, by the relevant academic departments. MIT will not give
  prospective students a credit assessment.
- Where the record does not merit credit, you may sit an **Advanced Standing Examination**.

## Testing and deadlines
- **SAT or ACT required** (no ACT writing/science, no SAT essay). Tests must be taken **by
  October 31 for spring entry and before March 31 for fall entry**. All sittings must be reported;
  MIT superscores by section.
- **Spring entry (February): applications due October 15** — U.S. citizens and permanent residents only.
- **Fall entry (September): applications due March 1.** Financial aid applications due February 15.
- Materials: MIT Transfer Application form ($75), all secondary school + college transcripts,
  **two academic letters of recommendation from college instructors — one must be from a math or
  science instructor**, a School Official Report from an adviser/registrar. Optional creative portfolio.

## Majors requested
- **EECS** — Course 6. MIT's EECS degrees are 6-1 (Electrical Science and Engineering),
  6-2 (Electrical Engineering and Computer Science), 6-3 (Computer Science and Engineering),
  6-4 (Artificial Intelligence and Decision Making), 6-7, 6-9, 6-14.
- **Mathematics** — Course 18, including **18-C Mathematics with Computer Science**.
- **"Computational Science and Engineering"** — *** MIT has no undergraduate CSE major. ***
  The Center for Computational Science and Engineering is a graduate unit of the Schwarzman College
  of Computing. The undergraduate options closest to the user's intent are **Course 6-3 (Computer
  Science and Engineering)** and **Course 18-C (Mathematics with Computer Science)**. Flag this.
- MIT admits to the Institute, not to a course; majors are declared at the end of the first year.
